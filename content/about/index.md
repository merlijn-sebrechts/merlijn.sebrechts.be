I'm a senior researcher at imec and teach at Ghent University, in the IDLab research group in Belgium. My work focuses on one question: **how can you trust the software and AI you depend on?** That covers where it runs, what is inside it, and who can see the data it processes.

I combine research, open source, and standardization work, and explain the results to engineers, decision makers, and the general public. [Contact me](mailto:merlijn.sebrechts@ugent.be) or see [Speaking & consulting](/talks/) if you’re looking for a speaker, expert insight, or research collaborations.

## What I work on

I lead a team working on cybersecurity and trust for software and AI. We work full stack, from chip to data platform.

* **Protecting data and software from physical and digital attacks.** Keeping sensitive data and algorithms safe, even on hardware or in clouds you don't control, using technologies such as confidential computing.
* **AI supply chain insight & trust.** Knowing what goes into software and AI models, and what that means for European legislation such as the Cyber Resilience Act. We use tools like Software and AI Bills of Materials (SBOM/AIBOM) to make that visible.
* **Trustworthy & robust distributed systems.** Deploying and managing software and AI across clouds and devices, and isolating it so attacks can't spread and AI agents stay within their bounds.

Most of my impact today comes through my team. I supervise seven PhD researchers and manage international research projects with industry and academia, such as the EU projects [ELASTIC](https://elasticproject.eu/), [FOCAL](https://www.focal-pqc.eu/), and [CRACY](https://cra-cy.eu/). My job is to set the direction, find the funding and partners, and help each researcher grow into an independent expert.

A second part of my job is to **teach at Ghent University**. I'm lecturer-in-charge of *Distributed Systems Design* and teach courses on cloud computing and software engineering. I also give guest lectures on open source software, GDPR, WebAssembly, and more.
